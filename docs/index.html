Condition monitoring · pumps · simulation

A simulated pump gets faults injected, and a classifier spots them. A simple rule almost matches it.

On 24 simulated runs the model had never seen, a random forest was right for 99.3 percent of readings. A plain rule (“any sensor is far outside its normal range”) reached 98.4 percent.

All data is generated by the project’s own simulation. There is no real pump data here.

What the numbers show

How it works

  1. A small simulation produces flow, pressure, vibration, temperature and power for a healthy pump and for three faults that start at step 700.
  2. A random forest learns, from 56 runs, which sensor patterns mean healthy and which mean faulty. It also sees rolling averages of the last 10 steps.
  3. It is scored on 24 other runs. The pump never leaves the computer: this is a teaching model of a digital twin, not a connected one.

Limits

  • The faults are added by hand as clean shifts. That is why a simple rule works almost as well. Real faults overlap, drift and differ from pump to pump.
  • The notebook also tried an LSTM network. It reached only 50 to 66 percent accuracy and was dropped for the random forest. Those results are kept in the notebook, not re-run here.
  • The health label flips at step 700, but the physical fault grows from zero, so the first steps after onset can not be told apart. This caps the achievable accuracy below 100 percent.
  • The old “live” dashboard needed a server. It was moved to the archive folder.
